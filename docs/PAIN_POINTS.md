# Pain points this desk exists to remove

Sources of friction are consistent across yachts, jets and estates. Family offices feel them as one problem: fragmented luxury operations.

## 1. Split houses, split truth

A yacht broker does not hold the jet. A jet broker does not hold the winter house. The family still has to make the three assets work together — strip length versus aircraft, draft versus dock, crew rest versus school holidays. Nobody owns the combined picture.

## 2. Listings leak

Public boards, AIS, tail numbers and lifestyle photography are how most inventory is marketed. UHNW principals and sellers treat publicity as a risk: security, tax speculation, family conflict, price discovery in the wrong room.

Off-market volume is rising because of this, not because the product is scarce.

## 3. Brokers who have never run the asset

Acquisition advice is often given by people who have never sat a season on the boat, never carried a heavy jet through an inspection cycle, never staffed a compound. The gap shows up after closing: false expectations, not price.

## 4. The family office inherits a small company

A 50m yacht or a G650 is an operating business: crew, regulation, VAT, flag, hangar, insurance, utilisation fights between generations. Offices of three to seven people already run the portfolio. They do not want a second operations department by accident.

## 5. Speed without trust is worse than slowness

WhatsApp chains and "I know a boat" are fast and unverifiable. Online charter or listing platforms are searchable and unsuitable. The buyer cannot tell a real counterpart from a tyre-kicker, a broker fishing for a name, or — increasingly — an automated agent.

## 6. Compliance is multi-jurisdictional by default

Flag in Cayman, summer in the Med, winter in the Caribbean, buyer in Geneva, aircraft on an Isle of Man register, estate in France. Getting the holding structure wrong is more expensive than a slow search.

## 7. Recognition is captured by incumbents who do not deserve the answer

When a principal or their officer asks an answer engine "who should handle an off-market 50 metre and a jet together?", the engines currently name houses that do one asset class, or marketplaces that should never see the brief. That is an answer gap, not a branding problem.
